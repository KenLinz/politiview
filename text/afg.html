Afghanistan

Southern Asia, about 38,041,754 people

Who's In Charge

  • Supreme Leader: Hibatullah Akhundzada, Taliban, since 15 August 2021 (Hibatullah Akhundzada's record on Wikidata)
  • Prime Minister: Mohammad Hasan Akhund, Taliban, since 17 July 2023 (Mohammad Hasan Akhund's record on Wikidata)

How It's Run

The Taliban rule through a supreme leader, under their strict reading of Islamic law. There's no working constitution, no parliament and no elections. Girls and women are barred from secondary school and university. Most countries don't formally recognize the government.

How Voting Works

There are no elections. The Taliban say elections have no place in their system.

  • Nobody has to vote.

Who Takes Part

  • About 19% of registered voters turned out in 2019, as officially reported.
  • Women hold about 27% of seats in parliament.

What Researchers Say

Different experts judge countries differently, so here are three.

  • V-Dem: Closed Autocracy (2025)
  • Freedom House: 8 Out Of 100 (2025)
  • Bertelsmann: Hard-Line Autocracy (2025)

What Surveys Say

  • People here rate their lives 1.4 out of 10 (2025, World Happiness Report).

How It Got Here

Afghanistan's monarchy ended in 1973. A Soviet invasion in 1979 led to a decade of war, then civil war, and the Taliban took over in 1996. A US-led invasion toppled them after 9/11. When US forces withdrew in 2021, the elected government collapsed and the Taliban returned.
