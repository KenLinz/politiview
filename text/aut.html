Austria

Western Europe, about 8,877,067 people

Who's In Charge

  • Chancellor: Christian Stocker, Austrian People's Party, since 3 March 2025 (Christian Stocker's record on Wikidata)
  • President: Alexander Van der Bellen, Independent, since 26 January 2017 (Alexander Van der Bellen's record on Wikidata)

How It's Run

Voters elect the National Council, and a chancellor runs the government. A president, also elected by voters, is mostly for show but keeps some emergency powers. Austria has nine states, each with powers of its own.

How Voting Works

Seats are shared by party vote share, with a 4% minimum to get in. Governments are usually coalitions. Austrians can vote from age 16.

  • Voting age: 16.
  • People vote about every 5 years.
  • Nobody has to vote.

What Surveys Say

  • People here rate their lives 6.8 out of 10 (2025, World Happiness Report).
  • About 73% said they trust their national government (2020, Wellcome Global Monitor).

How It Got Here

Austria was the heart of the vast Habsburg Empire, which collapsed after World War I. Germany annexed it in 1938. After World War II, the Allies occupied it until 1955, when it became independent and committed to neutrality.
