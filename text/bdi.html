Burundi

Eastern Africa, about 11,530,580 people

Who's In Charge

  • President: Évariste Ndayishimiye, National Council for the Defense of Democracy–Forces for the Defense of Democracy, since 18 June 2020 (Évariste Ndayishimiye's record on Wikidata)
  • Prime Minister: Nestor Ntahontuye (Nestor Ntahontuye's record on Wikidata)

The Next Vote

President, 3 May 2027.

How It's Run

A president runs the government, and the ruling party, the CNDD-FDD, dominates. The constitution shares posts between Hutu and Tutsi.

How Voting Works

Elections are held, but observers and rights groups say the opposition faces tight limits and threats. The main opposition party was in effect shut out of the 2025 vote for parliament. In 2015, the president's bid for a third term set off unrest.

  • Voting age: 18.
  • People vote about every 5 years.
  • Nobody has to vote.

How It Got Here

Burundi was a kingdom, then a German and Belgian colony, and became independent in 1962. A coup in 1966 ended the monarchy. Conflict between its Hutu majority and Tutsi minority led to massacres and a civil war from 1993 to 2005.
