Belgium

Western Europe, about 11,484,055 people

Who's In Charge

  • Prime Minister: Bart De Wever, New Flemish Alliance, since 3 February 2025 (Bart De Wever's record on Wikidata)
  • King: Philippe of Belgium, since 21 July 2013 (Philippe of Belgium's record on Wikidata)

How It's Run

Voters elect a federal parliament, and a group of parties picks a prime minister. The king's job is mostly for show. Power is shared with regions and language communities, because Dutch-speaking Flanders and French-speaking Wallonia have separate parties and politics.

How Voting Works

Seats are shared by party vote share, in districts. Voting is compulsory, so turnout is very high. Because each language group votes for its own parties, forming a government can be very slow: one attempt, in 2010 and 2011, took 541 days.

  • Voting age: 18.
  • People vote about every 5 years.
  • By law, everyone has to vote.

What Surveys Say

  • People here rate their lives 6.9 out of 10 (2025, World Happiness Report).
  • About 55% said they trust their national government (2020, Wellcome Global Monitor).

How It Got Here

Belgium broke away from the Netherlands in 1830. Tensions between its language groups grew over time, and from the 1970s the country was gradually reorganised into a federal state to manage them.
