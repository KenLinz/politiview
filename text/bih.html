Bosnia and Herzegovina

Southern Europe, about 3,301,000 people

Who's In Charge

  • Chairwoman: Borjana Krišto, Croatian Democratic Union of Bosnia and Herzegovina, since 28 December 2022 (Borjana Krišto's record on Wikidata)
  • Presidency: Denis Bećirović, Social Democratic Party of Bosnia and Herzegovina, since 16 November 2022 (Denis Bećirović's record on Wikidata)

How It's Run

Bosnia and Herzegovina has one of the world's most complicated systems. Three people share the presidency: one Bosniak, one Croat and one Serb. The country has two largely self-ruling parts, the Federation and Republika Srpska. An international official, the High Representative, has disputed powers to impose laws and remove officials.

How Voting Works

Voters in the Federation elect the Bosniak and Croat presidents, and voters in Republika Srpska elect the Serb one. Seats in the state parliament are also split among the three main groups. European judges have ruled it's discrimination to bar people outside these groups, such as Jews and Roma, from some top jobs.

  • Voting age: 18.
  • People vote about every 4 years.
  • Nobody has to vote.

Who Takes Part

  • About 52% of registered voters turned out in 2022, as officially reported.
  • Women hold about 19% of seats in parliament.

What Surveys Say

  • People here rate their lives 6.4 out of 10 (2025, World Happiness Report).
  • About 25% said they trust their national government (2020, Wellcome Global Monitor).

How It Got Here

War followed the breakup of Yugoslavia, from 1992 to 1995. International courts have ruled that the 1995 killings at Srebrenica were genocide, a finding some Bosnian Serb leaders reject. The Dayton Agreement ended the fighting in 1995 and set up this power-sharing system to keep the peace.
