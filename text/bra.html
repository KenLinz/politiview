Brazil

South America, about 211,049,527 people

Who's In Charge

  • President: Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, Workers' Party, since 1 January 2023 (Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva's record on Wikidata)

How It's Run

Voters elect a president, who runs the government, and a National Congress with two chambers. Brazil is a huge country of 26 states and a federal district, and each has powers of its own.

How Voting Works

If no one wins a majority for president, the top two go to a runoff. Voting is compulsory for adults, and uses electronic machines. So many parties win seats that presidents must always build coalitions.

Who Takes Part

  • About 79% of registered voters turned out in 2022, as officially reported.
  • Women hold about 18% of seats in parliament.

What Researchers Say

Different experts judge countries differently, so here are three.

  • V-Dem: Electoral Democracy (2025)
  • Freedom House: 73 Out Of 100 (2025)
  • Bertelsmann: Defective Democracy (2025)

What Surveys Say

  • People here rate their lives 6.6 out of 10 (2025, World Happiness Report).
  • About 41% said they trust their national government (2020, Wellcome Global Monitor).

How It Got Here

Portugal made Brazil a colony. It became an independent empire in 1822 and a republic in 1889. It was the last country in the Americas to end slavery, in 1888. An army dictatorship ruled from 1964 to 1985. Today's constitution dates from 1988.

Who It Stands With

  • BRICS: Large emerging powers seeking more say in how the world is run.
