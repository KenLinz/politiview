Canada

Northern America, about 37,589,262 people

Who's In Charge

  • Prime Minister: Mark Carney, Liberal Party of Canada, since 14 March 2025 (Mark Carney's record on Wikidata)
  • King: Charles III, since 8 September 2022 (Charles III's record on Wikidata)

How It's Run

Voters elect a parliament. The leader of the party that can win its support becomes prime minister. King Charles is head of state, but his job is mostly for show, and a governor general stands in for him. Ten provinces have strong powers of their own, including over health and schools.

How Voting Works

Canadians vote in 343 local areas called ridings. Whoever gets the most votes in a riding wins it, which is first-past-the-post. The Senate isn't elected: its members are appointed. The constitution allows up to five years between elections, a law sets four, and they're often called sooner.

  • Voting age: 18.
  • People vote about every 4 years.
  • Nobody has to vote.

  • People here rate their lives 6.7 out of 10 (2025, World Happiness Report).
  • About 73% said they trust their national government (2020, Wellcome Global Monitor).

How It Got Here

Indigenous nations already lived here when France and Britain both set up colonies. Britain took control in 1763. In 1867 several colonies joined to form a self-governing country under the Crown. Canada got full control of its own constitution only in 1982. Quebec, which mostly speaks French, has voted twice on leaving. It stayed both times.

Who It Stands With

  • The Commonwealth: Mostly former parts of the British Empire, tied by history and language.
