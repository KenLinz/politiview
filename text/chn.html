China

Eastern Asia, about 1,397,715,000 people

Who's In Charge

  • President: Xi Jinping, Chinese Communist Party, since 14 March 2013 (Xi Jinping's record on Wikidata)
  • Premier: Li Qiang, Chinese Communist Party, since 11 March 2023 (Li Qiang's record on Wikidata)

How It's Run

The Chinese Communist Party runs the country, and its top leader, the General Secretary, holds the most power. The government, courts and army all answer to the party. Xi Jinping is also president, and in 2018 the limit on a president's terms was removed.

How Voting Works

There are no national elections where voters choose between parties. People can vote for local representatives, usually from approved candidates. Those bodies choose the level above them, up to the National People's Congress. The government calls this a different kind of democracy. Critics say it gives no real choice.

  • Voting age: 18.
  • People vote about every 5 years.
  • Nobody has to vote.

Who Takes Part

  • Women hold about 27% of seats in parliament.

Different experts judge countries differently, so here are three.

  • V-Dem: Closed Autocracy (2025)
  • Freedom House: 9 Out Of 100 (2025)
  • Bertelsmann: Hard-Line Autocracy (2025)

What Surveys Say

  • People here rate their lives 6.1 out of 10 (2025, World Happiness Report).

How It Got Here

China's last dynasty fell in 1912. Nationalists and Communists fought a long civil war. In 1949 Mao Zedong declared the People's Republic, and the Nationalists fell back to Taiwan. After Mao, Deng Xiaoping opened up the economy from 1978 but kept party rule. The army ended pro-democracy protests in Tiananmen Square in 1989.

Who It Stands With
