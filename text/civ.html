Ivory Coast

Western Africa, about 25,716,544 people

Who's In Charge

  • President: Alassane Ouattara, Rally of Houphouëtists for Democracy and Peace, since 4 December 2010 (Alassane Ouattara's record on Wikidata)
  • Prime Minister: Robert Beugré Mambé, since 16 October 2023 (Robert Beugré Mambé's record on Wikidata)

How It's Run

A strong president runs the government. Alassane Ouattara won a fourth term in 2025 after courts barred some leading rivals. Critics called that unfair, and officials said it followed the rules.

How Voting Works

A president needs a majority, or there's a runoff. Elections have often been tense, and disputes have turned violent.

  • Voting age: 18.
  • People vote about every 5 years.
  • Nobody has to vote.

Who Takes Part

  • About 50% of registered voters turned out in 2025, as officially reported.
  • Women hold about 15% of seats in parliament.

What Researchers Say

Different experts judge countries differently, so here are three.

  • Freedom House: 46 Out Of 100 (2025)

What Surveys Say

  • People here rate their lives 5.2 out of 10 (2025, World Happiness Report).
  • About 40% said they trust their national government (2020, Wellcome Global Monitor).

How It Got Here

Under Félix Houphouët-Boigny, it was stable and well-off for the region for decades after independence from France in 1960. Civil wars followed from 2002 to 2007 and from 2010 to 2011, the second set off by a disputed election.

Who It Stands With

  • African Union: Nearly every country in Africa, working together on peace and politics.
