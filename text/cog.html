Republic of the Congo

Middle Africa, about 5,380,508 people

Who's In Charge

  • President: Denis Sassou-Nguesso, Congolese Party of Labour, since 25 October 1997 (Denis Sassou-Nguesso's record on Wikidata)
  • Prime Minister: Anatole Collinet Makosso, Congolese Party of Labour, since 12 May 2021 (Anatole Collinet Makosso's record on Wikidata)

How It's Run

Denis Sassou Nguesso has led for most of the time since 1979, and a 2015 constitutional change let him continue. His party, the Congolese Labour Party, dominates.

How Voting Works

Elections are held, but rights groups say opposition figures have been jailed and the internet has been cut during voting. In March 2026 he was announced the winner with about 95 percent, after major opposition parties refused to take part.

  • Voting age: 18.
  • People vote about every 5 years.
  • Nobody has to vote.

Who Takes Part

  • About 67% of registered voters turned out in 2021, as officially reported.
  • Women hold about 15% of seats in parliament.

What Researchers Say

Different experts judge countries differently, so here are three.

  • Freedom House: 17 Out Of 100 (2025)

What Surveys Say

  • People here rate their lives 4.5 out of 10 (2025, World Happiness Report).
  • About 52% said they trust their national government (2020, Wellcome Global Monitor).

How It Got Here

It won independence from France in 1960, and was a Marxist-Leninist state with one party from 1969. Elections with many parties began in 1992. A civil war in 1997 brought Sassou Nguesso back to power.

Who It Stands With

  • African Union: Nearly every country in Africa, working together on peace and politics.
