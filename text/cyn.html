How It's Run

Northern Cyprus is a self-declared state in the north of the island of Cyprus. It has an elected president and parliament, and a prime minister who runs the government. Only Turkey recognizes it as a country. The Republic of Cyprus, the UN and nearly all other countries see the area as part of Cyprus under Turkish military occupation. Turkey and Turkish Cypriot leaders say Turkey's troops are there under a 1960 treaty, and that Turkish Cypriots have a right to rule themselves.

How Voting Works

Voters elect a president and a parliament called the Assembly of the Republic. Turkish Cypriot officials run these elections and describe the state as independent. Almost no other country recognizes the state, so the wider world doesn't recognize its elections.

  • Voting age: 18.
  • People vote about every 5 years.

How It Got Here

After Cyprus became independent in 1960, violence broke out between Greek and Turkish Cypriots. In 1974 a coup backed by Greece's military government was followed by a Turkish military operation. The island has been split ever since. Turkish Cypriots declared their own republic in 1983, and the UN Security Council called that legally invalid. Talks to reunite the island have failed so far.
