The Dictionary

Political words, in plain English.

A B C D E F G H I J L M O P R S T U V

A

Absolute monarchy
A country where the monarch holds most of the power, and isn't limited by elected officials.
Autocracy
A system where one person or a small group holds power, and ordinary people can't remove them by voting.

B

Ballot
The paper or screen where a voter marks their choice, or the act of voting itself.
Bicameral
A parliament with two chambers.

C

Cabinet
The group of senior ministers who run the big government departments.
Candidate
A person who is trying to win an election.
Canton
A Swiss state. Swiss cantons have a lot of power of their own.
Caucus
A meeting of party members to choose candidates or make decisions. The word can also mean a group of politicians from one party or with a shared aim.
Ceremonial
A role that's mostly for big occasions and symbols, without real power over decisions.
Chancellor
The title for the head of government in Germany and Austria, like a prime minister.
Civil war
A war between groups within the same country.
Cleric
A religious leader, such as a priest or imam.
Coalition
When several parties team up to govern, because none won enough seats alone.
Colony
A territory ruled by a distant country, which is often used for its land and resources.
Compulsory voting
When the law requires eligible people to vote, sometimes with a small fine if they don't.
Constituency
An area whose voters elect their own representative. Also called a district, electorate or riding.
Constitution
The top set of rules for a country. It says who has what power, and other laws have to follow it.
Constitutional monarchy
A country that keeps a king or queen as head of state, while the monarch's powers are limited by law and elected politicians usually do the governing.
Coup
A sudden, illegal takeover of government, often by the military.

D

Delegate
A person chosen to speak and vote for a larger group, such as at a party meeting that picks a candidate.
Democracy
A system where the people choose who governs them, usually by voting, and can vote them out again.
Dictatorship
Rule by one person with no real limits on their power.

E

Electoral college
A group of people chosen to formally pick a leader. The US has 538 electors who choose the president, and in most states all of a state's electors go to whoever wins that state. Other countries use electoral colleges too.
Emir
A ruler in some Arab countries, a bit like a king.

F

Far left
A label for parties well to the left of most others, often wanting big changes to who owns what. Many parties given this label reject it.
Far right
A label for parties well to the right of most others, often very strict on immigration and national identity. Many parties given this label reject it.
Federal
A system where power is shared between the national government and states or provinces, each with powers of their own.
Federation
A country made up of states or provinces that each have powers of their own.
Filibuster
Talking or using the rules to delay a vote in a lawmaking body, so a bill can't pass unless enough members agree to end the delay.
First-past-the-post
A voting system where whoever gets the most votes in an area wins its seat, even without a majority.

G

Gerrymandering
Drawing election district borders to help one side win more seats.
Governor general
The person who stands in for the British monarch in countries like Canada and Australia. The role is mostly ceremonial.

H

Head of government
The person who actually runs the government day to day.
Head of state
The person who officially represents the country, like a president or monarch. In some places the job is mostly symbolic, and in others it comes with real power.
Hereditary
Passed down through a family, from parent to child.

I

Impeachment
A formal process in which a legislature accuses a top official of serious wrongdoing, which can lead to removal from office.
Incumbent
The person who currently holds an office and is trying to keep it in the next election.
Independence
When a place stops being ruled by another country and governs itself.
Interim
Temporary. An interim leader holds the job until a permanent one is chosen.

J

Junta
A government run by a small group of military officers, usually after they took power by force.

L

Legislature
The part of government that makes the laws.
Lower house
In a parliament with two chambers, the one that is usually larger and usually more powerful. It is usually elected directly.

M

Majority
More than half.
Martial law
When the army takes over running things, usually in an emergency, and normal rights are put on hold.
Midterm
In the US, elections held halfway through a president's four-year term, for Congress and many state offices.
Minister
A senior politician in charge of one area of government, like health or defense.
Mixed-member
A voting system that combines local district winners with seats given out by party list. Sometimes the list seats make the result more proportional, and sometimes they don't.
Monarchy
A country with a king, queen, emperor or similar ruler who usually inherits the title.

O

One-party state
A country where one political party holds all real power, and other parties are banned or only allowed as junior partners.
Opposition
The parties that aren't in government, whose job includes questioning and challenging those that are. In some countries they have little freedom to do this.

P

Parliament
A group of people who meet to make a country's laws, usually elected. Different countries call it different things, like Congress or the Diet.
Parliamentary
A system where voters elect a parliament, and the parliament then decides who runs the government.
Party list
A ranked list of a party's candidates. If a party wins ten seats, the top ten people on its list get them.
Plurality
The most votes of anyone, even if it's less than half.
President
A leader who may either run the government or be mostly ceremonial, depending on the country.
Presidential
A system where the president is chosen separately from the people who write the laws, and runs the government. Most often voters elect the president, directly or through a special group of electors.
Primary
A vote within a party to choose its candidate for an election. Primaries are used in the US and a few other places.
Prime minister
The leader of the government in many countries. Usually chosen by parliament or the ruling party rather than directly by voters.
Proportional representation
A voting system where parties get seats in proportion to their votes. Win 30% of the votes, get about 30% of the seats.
Protectorate
A place that keeps its own rulers while a stronger country controls its defense and foreign affairs.

R

Ranked choice
A voting system where you rank candidates in order, and your vote moves to your next choice if your first is eliminated.
Referendum
A vote where the people directly decide on a single question, instead of choosing a person.
Republic
A country where the head of state is chosen, not born into the job.
Runoff
A second vote between the top candidates, held when nobody won enough the first time.

S

Seat
One place in a parliament or other elected body. A party that wins 30 seats has 30 members there.
Sectarian
Divided along the lines of religious groups.
Secular
Kept separate from religion.
Semi-presidential
A system with both a president and a prime minister who share power. Voters usually elect the president.
Senate
A name often used for the upper chamber of a parliament. Some senators are elected, and some are appointed.
Sharia
Islamic religious law, which some countries use as part of their legal system.
Suffrage
The right to vote.
Sultan
A ruler in some Muslim countries, a bit like a king.
Supreme court
The highest court in a country. It settles the hardest legal questions, and in many countries it can strike down laws that break the constitution.
Swing
A shift in votes from one party to another between elections. A swing seat or state is one that could go either way.

T

Term limit
A rule on how long or how many times someone can hold a job.
Theocracy
A government where religious leaders or religious law hold the highest authority.
Threshold
The minimum share of votes a party needs before it gets any seats.
Tribal
Relating to traditional groups based on family ties and shared ancestry.
Turnout
The share of eligible voters who actually vote in an election.
Two-round
A voting system where, if nobody gets a majority in the first vote, the top candidates face each other in a second vote.

U

Unicameral
A parliament with just one chamber.
Unitary
A system where the national government holds most power, and regions only have what it gives them.
Upper house
In a parliament with two chambers, the second chamber. It often reviews laws and often represents regions, but its powers differ a lot between countries.

V

Veto
The power to say no to a decision or law so that it can't go ahead. In some countries it can be overridden.
Voting age
How old you have to be to vote. In most countries it's 18, and in some it's 16 or 17.
