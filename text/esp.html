Spain

Southern Europe, about 47,076,781 people

Who's In Charge

  • Prime Minister: Pedro Sánchez, Spanish Socialist Workers' Party, since 2 June 2018 (Pedro Sánchez's record on Wikidata)
  • King: Felipe VI of Spain, since 19 June 2014 (Felipe VI of Spain's record on Wikidata)

The Next Vote

Senate and Congress of Deputies, 29 November 2026.

How It's Run

Voters elect the Congress of Deputies, which chooses a prime minister. The king is head of state, but his job is mostly for show. Seventeen regions, such as Catalonia and the Basque Country, largely run their own affairs.

How Voting Works

Parties share out seats in the Congress by their votes in each province, and need at least 3% there. Small provinces get more seats per voter, which helps the biggest parties and regional parties.

  • Voting age: 18.
  • People vote about every 4 years.
  • Nobody has to vote.

What Surveys Say

  • People here rate their lives 6.5 out of 10 (2025, World Happiness Report).
  • About 48% said they trust their national government (2020, Wellcome Global Monitor).

How It Got Here

A civil war from 1936 to 1939 ended with General Franco ruling as a dictator until he died in 1975. King Juan Carlos then helped lead a peaceful move to democracy, and the 1978 constitution created today's system. Catalonia's movement for independence is still a big issue.
