Ethiopia

Eastern Africa, about 112,078,730 people

Who's In Charge

  • Prime Minister: Abiy Ahmed, Prosperity Party, since 1 April 2018 (Abiy Ahmed's record on Wikidata)
  • President: Taye Atske Selassie, since 7 October 2024 (Taye Atske Selassie's record on Wikidata)

How It's Run

Voters elect a House of Peoples' Representatives. The prime minister, who leads the largest party, runs the government. Ethiopia's regions are largely organized around ethnic groups, and the president's job is mostly for show.

How Voting Works

Whoever gets the most votes in each local district wins. The ruling Prosperity Party won a large majority in June 2026, and parts of the country, including all of Tigray, could not vote because of conflict.

  • Voting age: 18.
  • People vote about every 5 years.
  • Nobody has to vote.

The Last Vote For The Top Job

Who Takes Part

  • About 94% of registered voters turned out in 2021, as officially reported.
  • Women hold about 42% of seats in parliament.

What Researchers Say

Different experts judge countries differently, so here are three.

  • V-Dem: Electoral Autocracy (2025)
  • Freedom House: 18 Out Of 100 (2025)
  • Bertelsmann: Hard-Line Autocracy (2025)

What Surveys Say

  • People here rate their lives 4.0 out of 10 (2025, World Happiness Report).
  • About 78% said they trust their national government (2020, Wellcome Global Monitor).

How It Got Here

Never fully colonized, Ethiopia was ruled by emperors until the military deposed Haile Selassie in 1974 and set up a Marxist government. Rebel forces took power in 1991. Abiy Ahmed won the Nobel Peace Prize in 2019, and a war in Tigray followed from 2020 to 2022.
