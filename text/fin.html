Finland

Northern Europe, about 5,520,314 people

Who's In Charge

  • Prime Minister: Petteri Orpo, National Coalition Party, since 20 June 2023 (Petteri Orpo's record on Wikidata)
  • President: Alexander Stubb, Independent, since 1 March 2024 (Alexander Stubb's record on Wikidata)

The Next Vote

Parliament, 18 April 2027.

How It's Run

Voters elect a parliament, and the prime minister runs the government. A president, also elected by voters, shares foreign policy with the government. Most governments are broad coalitions.

How Voting Works

Voters choose a specific candidate from party lists, and seats are shared by party vote share. In 1906 Finland became the first country in Europe to give women the vote.

  • Voting age: 18.
  • People vote about every 4 years.
  • Nobody has to vote.

  • People here rate their lives 7.8 out of 10 (2025, World Happiness Report).
  • About 81% said they trust their national government (2020, Wellcome Global Monitor).

How It Got Here

Finland was part of Sweden for centuries, then part of Russia. It became independent in 1917 after the Russian Revolution. It fought the Soviet Union in World War II, stayed neutral in the Cold War and joined NATO in 2023.

Who It Stands With

  • NATO: A military alliance. Members promise to defend one another.
  • European Union: European countries that make many of their laws together.
