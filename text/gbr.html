United Kingdom

Northern Europe, about 66,834,405 people

Who's In Charge

  • Prime Minister: Andy Burnham, Labour Party, since 20 July 2026 (Andy Burnham's record on Wikidata)
  • King: Charles III, since 8 September 2022 (Charles III's record on Wikidata)

How It's Run

Voters elect a parliament. Whoever can win its support becomes prime minister and runs the government. King Charles is head of state, but his job is mostly for show. The UK has no single written constitution. Its rules come from laws, court decisions and long-held customs.

How Voting Works

The country is split into 650 areas, and each elects one MP: whoever gets the most votes wins, even under half, which is called first-past-the-post. So a party can win most of the seats with well under half the votes. The upper chamber, the House of Lords, isn't elected. Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland have their own parliaments or assemblies as well.

  • Voting age: 18.
  • People vote about every 5 years.
  • Nobody has to vote.

How It Got Here

Power moved slowly from kings to parliament, through Magna Carta in 1215, a civil war in the 1640s, and the Glorious Revolution of 1688. Voting spread step by step, starting with the Reform Act of 1832 and reaching all adult women in 1928. Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland gained their own parliaments in the late 1990s.
