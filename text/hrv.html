Croatia

Southern Europe, about 4,067,500 people

Who's In Charge

  • Prime Minister: Andrej Plenković, Croatian Democratic Union, since 19 October 2016 (Andrej Plenković's record on Wikidata)
  • President: Zoran Milanović, Social Democratic Party of Croatia, since 19 February 2020 (Zoran Milanović's record on Wikidata)

How It's Run

Voters elect a parliament, and the prime minister runs the government. A president, also elected by voters, has limited powers, including over the military.

How Voting Works

Parties share out seats by their votes across districts. Some seats are saved for minorities and for Croatians living abroad.

  • Voting age: 18.
  • People vote about every 4 years.
  • Nobody has to vote.

Who Takes Part

  • About 44% of registered voters turned out in 2025, as officially reported.
  • Women hold about 33% of seats in parliament.

Different experts judge countries differently, so here are three.

  • V-Dem: Electoral Democracy (2025)
  • Freedom House: 82 Out Of 100 (2025)
  • Bertelsmann: Consolidating Democracy (2025)

What Surveys Say

  • People here rate their lives 6.0 out of 10 (2025, World Happiness Report).
  • About 42% said they trust their national government (2020, Wellcome Global Monitor).

How It Got Here

Croatia declared independence from Yugoslavia in 1991, and fought a war with Serb-led forces until 1995. It joined the EU in 2013.

Who It Stands With

  • NATO: A military alliance. Members promise to defend one another.
  • European Union: European countries that make many of their laws together.
