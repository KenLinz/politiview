Ireland

Northern Europe, about 4,941,444 people

Who's In Charge

  • Taoiseach: Micheál Martin, Fianna Fáil, since 23 January 2025 (Micheál Martin's record on Wikidata)
  • President: Catherine Connolly, Independent, since 11 November 2025 (Catherine Connolly's record on Wikidata)

How It's Run

Voters elect a parliament called the Dáil. It chooses a prime minister, called the Taoiseach, who runs the government. A president is elected separately, but that job is mostly for show.

How Voting Works

Ireland uses a system called single transferable vote. You rank candidates in order of preference, each area elects several members, and your vote moves on if your favorite doesn't need it or can't win. It tends to give parties seats close to their share of the votes.

  • Voting age: 18.
  • People vote about every 5 years.
  • Nobody has to vote.

How It Got Here

Ireland was ruled by Britain for centuries. An uprising in 1916 and a war of independence led to a treaty in 1921 that created a self-governing Irish state, while Northern Ireland stayed in the UK. The treaty sparked a short civil war. Ireland became a republic in 1949, and the 1998 Good Friday Agreement ended most of the violence in the north.
