Iran

Southern Asia, about 82,913,906 people

Who's In Charge

  • Supreme Leader: Mojtaba Khamenei, Independent, since 8 March 2026 (Mojtaba Khamenei's record on Wikidata)
  • President: Masoud Pezeshkian, Independent, since 28 July 2024 (Masoud Pezeshkian's record on Wikidata)

How It's Run

Iran mixes elections with rule by clerics. A Supreme Leader, a senior cleric nobody votes for, has the final say over the army, the courts and major policy. An elected president runs daily government.

How Voting Works

People do vote for president and parliament. But a council of clerics and legal experts can block anyone from running, and it often removes reformers. So voters get a choice, but only among those approved.

  • Voting age: 18.
  • People vote about every 4 years.
  • Nobody has to vote.

What Researchers Say

Different experts judge countries differently, so here are three.

  • V-Dem: Electoral Autocracy (2025)
  • Freedom House: 10 Out Of 100 (2025)
  • Bertelsmann: Hard-Line Autocracy (2025)

What Surveys Say

  • People here rate their lives 5.2 out of 10 (2025, World Happiness Report).
  • About 46% said they trust their national government (2020, Wellcome Global Monitor).

How It Got Here

The US and Britain helped overthrow an elected prime minister in 1953, which gave the shah his power back. The 1979 Islamic Revolution, led by Ayatollah Khomeini, overthrew the shah and created today's system. Large protests have challenged it again and again since.
