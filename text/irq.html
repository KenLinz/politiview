Iraq

Western Asia, about 39,309,783 people

Who's In Charge

  • Prime Minister: Ali al-Zaidi, Independent (Ali al-Zaidi's record on Wikidata)
  • President: Nizar Amidi, Patriotic Union of Kurdistan, since 17 October 2022 (Nizar Amidi's record on Wikidata)

How It's Run

Voters elect a parliament, which elects a president whose job is mostly for show. The president names a prime minister, who holds the real power once parliament approves. By informal deal, top jobs are shared among groups: a Shia Arab prime minister, a Kurdish president and a Sunni Arab speaker.

How Voting Works

Seats are shared by vote share within provinces, with some reserved for women and minorities. Forming a government often takes months of negotiation.

  • Voting age: 18.
  • People vote about every 4 years.
  • Nobody has to vote.

What Surveys Say

  • People here rate their lives 5.2 out of 10 (2025, World Happiness Report).
  • About 34% said they trust their national government (2020, Wellcome Global Monitor).

How It Got Here

Britain drew Iraq's borders after World War I. Saddam Hussein ruled as a dictator from 1979, until a US-led invasion in 2003 overthrew him. Sectarian war followed, and the group ISIS seized large areas before being pushed back.
