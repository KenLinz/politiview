Japan

Eastern Asia, about 126,264,931 people

Who's In Charge

  • Prime Minister: Sanae Takaichi, Liberal Democratic Party, since 21 October 2025 (Sanae Takaichi's record on Wikidata)
  • Emperor: Naruhito, since 1 May 2019 (Naruhito's record on Wikidata)

How It's Run

Voters elect a parliament called the Diet, and it chooses a prime minister, who runs the government. The emperor is a symbol with no political power. One party, the Liberal Democrats, has governed for almost all of the time since 1955.

How Voting Works

Voters cast two ballots for the powerful lower house: one for a local candidate and one for a party. The two sets of seats are counted separately, so it's a mixed system without a top-up. The upper house has its own elections. The prime minister can call a lower-house election early.

  • Voting age: 18.
  • People vote about every 4 years.
  • Nobody has to vote.

  • About 50% said they trust their national government (2020, Wellcome Global Monitor).

How It Got Here

Japan was ruled by a militarist government and an empire until its defeat in World War II. During the US occupation, a new constitution in 1947 moved power from the emperor to the people and renounced war. Women voted for the first time in 1946.
