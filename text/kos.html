Kosovo

Southern Europe, about 1,794,248 people

Who's In Charge

  • Prime Minister: Albin Kurti, Vetëvendosje!, since 22 March 2021 (Albin Kurti's record on Wikidata)
  • President: Justina Shiroka Pula, Independent (Justina Shiroka Pula's record on Wikidata)

How It's Run

Voters elect the Assembly, and the prime minister runs the government. A president chosen by the Assembly is the head of state, and seats are reserved for Serb and other minorities. In 2026 the Assembly failed to elect a president, which led to early elections in June 2026.

How Voting Works

Of the 120 seats, 100 are shared by party vote share, and 20 are reserved for minorities, 10 of them for Serbs.

  • Voting age: 18.
  • People vote about every 4 years.
  • Nobody has to vote.

What Surveys Say

  • People here rate their lives 6.9 out of 10 (2025, World Happiness Report).
  • About 26% said they trust their national government (2020, Wellcome Global Monitor).

How It Got Here

Kosovo was part of Serbia, and most of its people are Albanian. A war in 1998 and 1999 ended with NATO bombing and UN rule. Kosovo declared independence in 2008. Many countries recognize it, but Serbia, Russia, China and others don't. Serbia sees Kosovo as part of its land.
