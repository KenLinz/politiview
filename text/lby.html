Libya

Northern Africa, about 6,777,452 people

Who's In Charge

  • Prime Minister: Abd al-Hamid Al-Dabiba, since 5 February 2021 (Abd al-Hamid Al-Dabiba's record on Wikidata)
  • Chairman: Mohamed al-Menfi, since 15 March 2021 (Mohamed al-Menfi's record on Wikidata)

How It's Run

Libya is split between rival governments. One in Tripoli is recognized by the UN. One in the east is backed by the elected House of Representatives and by military commander Khalifa Haftar. Armed groups hold much of the real power.

How Voting Works

National elections planned for December 2021 were postponed, and have not been held since. The last national parliament, the House of Representatives, was elected in 2014.

  • Voting age: 18.
  • Nobody has to vote.

Who Takes Part

  • About 50% of registered voters turned out in 2014, as officially reported.
  • Women hold about 17% of seats in parliament.

What Researchers Say

Different experts judge countries differently, so here are three.

  • Freedom House: 10 Out Of 100 (2025)

What Surveys Say

  • People here rate their lives 5.7 out of 10 (2025, World Happiness Report).

How It Got Here

Muammar Gaddafi ruled for 42 years after taking power in a 1969 coup. A 2011 uprising, backed by NATO air strikes, ended his rule and he was killed, and Libya has been divided and unstable since.

Who It Stands With

  • African Union: Nearly every country in Africa, working together on peace and politics.
  • Arab League: Arabic-speaking countries that coordinate on politics.
