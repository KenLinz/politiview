Liechtenstein

Western Europe, about 38,019 people

Who's In Charge

  • Prince: Hans-Adam II, Prince of Liechtenstein, since 13 November 1989 (Hans-Adam II, Prince of Liechtenstein's record on Wikidata)
  • Prime Minister: Brigitte Haas, Fatherland Union, since 10 April 2025 (Brigitte Haas's record on Wikidata)

How It's Run

An elected parliament and prime minister run the country, but the prince keeps strong powers. He can block laws and dismiss governments. Voters confirmed those powers in a 2003 vote.

How Voting Works

The 25 seats are shared by party vote share. Citizens can also force referendums. Women gained the vote only in 1984.

  • Voting age: 18.
  • People vote about every 4 years.
  • Nobody has to vote.

How It Got Here

The Liechtenstein family bought these lands around 1700, and they became a principality in 1719. It became independent when the German Confederation ended in 1866. Women won the vote in 1984.
