Sri Lanka

Southern Asia, about 21,803,000 people

Who's In Charge

  • President: Anura Kurankan Dissanayake, National People's Power, since 23 September 2024 (Anura Kurankan Dissanayake's record on Wikidata)

How It's Run

A president, elected by voters, holds major power, along with a parliament and a prime minister. Protests in 2022, during an economic collapse, forced the president to flee the country and resign.

How Voting Works

Voters rank up to three presidential candidates, and second choices count if nobody gets a majority. Parliament's seats are shared by party vote share.

  • Voting age: 18.
  • People vote about every 5 years.
  • Nobody has to vote.

Who Takes Part

  • About 75% of registered voters turned out in 2024, as officially reported.
  • Women hold about 10% of seats in parliament.

What Surveys Say

  • People here rate their lives 4.0 out of 10 (2025, World Happiness Report).
  • About 76% said they trust their national government (2020, Wellcome Global Monitor).

How It Got Here

Sri Lanka won independence from Britain in 1948. It fought a 26-year civil war against Tamil separatists, which ended in 2009 with many civilian deaths. In 2024, voters elected a left-wing candidate from outside the two old parties that had long led politics.
