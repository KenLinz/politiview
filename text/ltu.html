Lithuania

Northern Europe, about 2,786,844 people

Who's In Charge

  • President: Gitanas Nausėda, Independent, since 12 July 2019 (Gitanas Nausėda's record on Wikidata)
  • Prime Minister: Mindaugas Sinkevičius, Social Democratic Party of Lithuania, since 14 July 2026 (Mindaugas Sinkevičius's record on Wikidata)

How It's Run

A president, elected by voters, leads foreign policy. A prime minister, backed by parliament, runs daily government.

How Voting Works

The Seimas is mixed: 71 members win local districts, and 70 seats are shared by party vote share. The president is elected in two rounds, if needed.

  • Voting age: 18.
  • People vote about every 4 years.
  • Nobody has to vote.

What Surveys Say

  • People here rate their lives 6.7 out of 10 (2025, World Happiness Report).
  • About 55% said they trust their national government (2020, Wellcome Global Monitor).

How It Got Here

In the Middle Ages, Lithuania was one of Europe's largest states, and it was joined with Poland for centuries. It became independent in 1918, and the Soviet Union took it over in 1940. In 1990 it was the first Soviet republic to declare independence again.

Who It Stands With
