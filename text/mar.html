Morocco

Northern Africa, about 36,471,769 people

Who's In Charge

  • King: Mohammed VI of Morocco, since 23 July 1999 (Mohammed VI of Morocco's record on Wikidata)
  • Prime Minister: Fatima Ezzahra El Mansouri, Authenticity and Modernity Party, since 29 September 2026 (Fatima Ezzahra El Mansouri's record on Wikidata)

How It's Run

The king holds the most power, especially over religion, security and foreign affairs. Voters elect a House of Representatives, and the king appoints a head of government from the party with the most seats.

How Voting Works

Parties share out seats in the House of Representatives by their votes, with lists saved for women and young people. Many parties compete. Critics say they govern within limits the palace sets. The monarchy says the 2011 constitution, written after protests, gave parliament and the government real powers. The upper house isn't directly elected.

  • Voting age: 18.
  • People vote about every 5 years.
  • Nobody has to vote.

Who Takes Part

  • About 50% of registered voters turned out in 2021, as officially reported.
  • Women hold about 27% of seats in parliament.

What Researchers Say

Different experts judge countries differently, so here are three.

  • V-Dem: Closed Autocracy (2025)
  • Freedom House: 37 Out Of 100 (2025)
  • Bertelsmann: Hard-Line Autocracy (2025)

What Surveys Say

  • People here rate their lives 4.7 out of 10 (2025, World Happiness Report).
  • About 45% said they trust their national government (2020, Wellcome Global Monitor).

How It Got Here

The Alaouite family has ruled since the 1600s, and Morocco became independent from France and Spain in 1956. Morocco says Western Sahara is part of its land and controls most of it. The Polisario Front and others dispute this.

Who It Stands With

  • African Union: Nearly every country in Africa, working together on peace and politics.
  • Arab League: Arabic-speaking countries that coordinate on politics.
