North Macedonia

Southern Europe, about 2,083,459 people

Who's In Charge

  • Prime Minister: Hristijan Mickoski, Internal Macedonian Revolutionary Organization – Democratic Party for Macedonian National Unity, since 23 June 2024 (Hristijan Mickoski's record on Wikidata)
  • President: Gordana Siljanovska-Davkova, Internal Macedonian Revolutionary Organization – Democratic Party for Macedonian National Unity, since 12 May 2024 (Gordana Siljanovska-Davkova's record on Wikidata)

How It's Run

Voters elect a parliament, and the prime minister runs the government. A president, also elected by voters, is mostly for show.

How Voting Works

Seats are shared by party vote share. Power-sharing rules, from a 2001 peace deal, ensure a voice for the large Albanian minority.

  • Voting age: 18.
  • People vote about every 4 years.
  • Nobody has to vote.

Who Takes Part

  • About 55% of registered voters turned out in 2024, as officially reported.
  • Women hold about 39% of seats in parliament.

What Researchers Say

Different experts judge countries differently, so here are three.

  • V-Dem: Electoral Democracy (2025)
  • Freedom House: 67 Out Of 100 (2025)
  • Bertelsmann: Defective Democracy (2025)

What Surveys Say

  • People here rate their lives 5.7 out of 10 (2025, World Happiness Report).
  • About 37% said they trust their national government (2020, Wellcome Global Monitor).

How It Got Here

It left Yugoslavia peacefully in 1991. Greece objected to its name for decades, and a 2018 deal changed it to North Macedonia, opening the door to NATO, which it joined in 2020.

Who It Stands With

  • NATO: A military alliance. Members promise to defend one another.
