Mongolia

Eastern Asia, about 3,225,167 people

Who's In Charge

  • President: Khurelsukh Ukhnaa, Mongolian People's Party, since 25 June 2021 (Khurelsukh Ukhnaa's record on Wikidata)
  • Prime Minister: Uchral Nyam-Osor, since 30 March 2026 (Uchral Nyam-Osor's record on Wikidata)

How It's Run

Voters elect a parliament, and the prime minister runs the government. A president, also elected by voters, can block some laws and has limited duties.

How Voting Works

A mixed system: some members win in local districts, and others by party vote share. Women make up a growing share under quota rules.

  • Voting age: 18.
  • People vote about every 4 years.
  • Nobody has to vote.

Who Takes Part

  • About 70% of registered voters turned out in 2024, as officially reported.
  • Women hold about 25% of seats in parliament.

What Researchers Say

Different experts judge countries differently, so here are three.

  • V-Dem: Electoral Democracy (2025)
  • Freedom House: 84 Out Of 100 (2025)
  • Bertelsmann: Defective Democracy (2025)

What Surveys Say

  • People here rate their lives 5.9 out of 10 (2025, World Happiness Report).
  • About 55% said they trust their national government (2020, Wellcome Global Monitor).

How It Got Here

Mongolia was once the center of Genghis Khan's empire. It became the world's second communist state in 1924, under Soviet influence. A peaceful revolution in 1990 made it a rare democracy between Russia and China.
