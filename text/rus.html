Russia

Eastern Europe, about 144,373,535 people

Who's In Charge

  • President: Vladimir Putin, since 7 May 2012 (Vladimir Putin's record on Wikidata)
  • Prime Minister: Mikhail Mishustin, Independent, since 16 January 2020 (Mikhail Mishustin's record on Wikidata)

How It's Run

On paper, Russia has an elected president and a parliament with two chambers. Researchers say real power sits with the president, and that the courts, parliament and most media follow his lead. A 2020 change to the constitution let the president then in office run for two more six-year terms. That could keep him in office until 2036.

How Voting Works

Voters elect the president every six years and the 450-seat State Duma, the main chamber of parliament. Opposition candidates are often barred or jailed. Critics and many foreign observers say this means there's no fair contest for power. The government says its elections are lawful. Researchers call Russia an 'authoritarian regime' or 'electoral autocracy': there are votes, but opposition is tightly limited.

  • Voting age: 18.
  • People vote about every 5 years.
  • Nobody has to vote.

How It Got Here

Tsars ruled Russia for centuries. The 1917 revolution led to a communist state, and the Soviet Union formed in 1922. It broke up in 1991, and Boris Yeltsin led a fast, rocky move to elections and free markets. The current president first took office in 2000. Critics say political competition has shrunk since then. Supporters credit the government with bringing back stability.
