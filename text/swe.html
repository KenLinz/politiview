Sweden

Northern Europe, about 10,285,453 people

Who's In Charge

  • Caretaker Prime Minister: Ulf Kristersson, Moderate Party, since 18 October 2022 (Ulf Kristersson's record on Wikidata)
  • King: Carl XVI Gustaf of Sweden, since 15 September 1973 (Carl XVI Gustaf of Sweden's record on Wikidata)

How It's Run

Voters elect the Riksdag, and the prime minister runs the government. The king has no political power at all. Since 1974 his job has been only for show.

How Voting Works

Seats are shared by party vote share, with a 4% minimum. Governments often rule without a majority, by making deals with other parties.

  • Voting age: 18.
  • People vote about every 4 years.
  • Nobody has to vote.

Who Takes Part

  • About 84% of registered voters turned out in 2022, as officially reported.
  • Women hold about 46% of seats in parliament.

What Researchers Say

Different experts judge countries differently, so here are three.

  • V-Dem: Liberal Democracy (2025)
  • Freedom House: 99 Out Of 100 (2025)

What Surveys Say

  • People here rate their lives 7.3 out of 10 (2025, World Happiness Report).
  • About 65% said they trust their national government (2020, Wellcome Global Monitor).

How It Got Here

Once a great military power, Sweden has avoided war since 1814. It built a large welfare state in the 20th century, and joined NATO in 2024, ending two centuries of non-alignment.

Who It Stands With

  • NATO: A military alliance. Members promise to defend one another.
  • European Union: European countries that make many of their laws together.
