Tunisia

Northern Africa, about 11,694,719 people

Who's In Charge

  • President: Kais Saied, Independent, since 23 October 2019 (Kais Saied's record on Wikidata)
  • Prime Minister: Sara Zaafarani (Sara Zaafarani's record on Wikidata)

How It's Run

President Kais Saied holds huge power. In 2021 he suspended parliament. He called that a lawful answer to a crisis, and opponents called it a self-coup. He then rewrote the constitution to gather power in the presidency. Rights groups say many opponents have been jailed.

How Voting Works

Elections are held, but very few people have voted. Observers and opposition groups say most serious rivals were barred or jailed before the 2024 vote for president, which the president won with over 90 percent.

  • Voting age: 18.
  • People vote about every 5 years.
  • Nobody has to vote.

What Researchers Say

Different experts judge countries differently, so here are three.

  • V-Dem: Electoral Autocracy (2025)
  • Freedom House: 42 Out Of 100 (2025)

What Surveys Say

  • People here rate their lives 4.8 out of 10 (2025, World Happiness Report).
  • About 26% said they trust their national government (2020, Wellcome Global Monitor).

How It Got Here

Tunisia's 2011 revolution removed Zine el-Abidine Ben Ali and began the Arab Spring. For a decade it was the region's one democratic success story. Economic troubles and political deadlock opened the way for Saied.

Who It Stands With
