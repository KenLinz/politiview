Turkey

Western Asia, about 83,429,615 people

Who's In Charge

  • President: Recep Tayyip Erdoğan, Justice and Development Party, since 28 August 2014 (Recep Tayyip Erdoğan's record on Wikidata)

How It's Run

A president, elected by voters, leads both the state and the government. A close vote adopted this system in 2017. There's no longer a prime minister, and the president's powers are broad.

How Voting Works

The president needs a majority, or there's a runoff. Parties share out seats in parliament by their votes. Elections are real contests, but some researchers rate Turkey an 'electoral autocracy', citing jailed opponents and tilted media, which the government rejects. Istanbul's opposition mayor was jailed in 2025.

  • Voting age: 18.
  • People vote about every 5 years.

How It Got Here

Mustafa Kemal Atatürk founded the secular republic in 1923, from the ruins of the Ottoman Empire. The army staged several coups, saying it was protecting the secular republic. Recep Tayyip Erdoğan's party took power in 2002. A failed coup in 2016 led to mass firings and arrests, which the government says targeted those behind the coup.
