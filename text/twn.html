Taiwan

Eastern Asia, about 23,568,378 people

Who's In Charge

  • President: Lai Ching-te, Democratic Progressive Party, since 20 May 2024 (Lai Ching-te's record on Wikidata)
  • Premier: Cho Jung-tai, Democratic Progressive Party, since 20 May 2024 (Cho Jung-tai's record on Wikidata)

How It's Run

Voters elect a president and a lawmaking body with one chamber. Taiwan runs itself, but its status is disputed. Its government says it's a sovereign state, officially called the Republic of China, while Beijing says Taiwan is part of China. Only about a dozen countries formally recognize it as a state.

How Voting Works

The president wins with the most votes, with no runoff. Lawmakers come from a mix of local winners and party-share seats. People here can also vote in national referendums.

  • Voting age: 20.
  • People vote about every 4 years.
  • Nobody has to vote.

How It Got Here

Japan ruled Taiwan from 1895 to 1945, after which the Republic of China took over. After losing China's civil war, the Nationalists fled to Taiwan in 1949, and ruled under martial law until 1987. The first direct presidential election was held in 1996. How to handle relations with China is its central political question.
