Tanzania

Eastern Africa, about 58,005,463 people

Who's In Charge

  • President: Samia Suluhu Hassan, Party of the Revolution, since 19 March 2021 (Samia Suluhu Hassan's record on Wikidata)

How It's Run

A president runs the government. One party, the CCM, and the party it grew from have held power since independence. Zanzibar has its own government within the union.

How Voting Works

The candidate for president with the most votes wins. In the October 2025 election, the two largest opposition parties were barred and foreign observers questioned the vote. Rights groups and others say protests were met with deadly force.

  • Voting age: 18.
  • People vote about every 5 years.
  • Nobody has to vote.

Who Takes Part

  • About 51% of registered voters turned out in 2020, as officially reported.
  • Women hold about 38% of seats in parliament.

What Researchers Say

Different experts judge countries differently, so here are three.

  • V-Dem: Electoral Autocracy (2025)
  • Freedom House: 28 Out Of 100 (2025)
  • Bertelsmann: Moderate Autocracy (2025)

What Surveys Say

  • People here rate their lives 3.9 out of 10 (2025, World Happiness Report).
  • About 90% said they trust their national government (2020, Wellcome Global Monitor).

How It Got Here

Tanganyika and Zanzibar joined together in 1964. The first president, Julius Nyerere, built a socialist state and a strong national identity. Politics with many parties began in the early 1990s.

Who It Stands With

  • African Union: Nearly every country in Africa, working together on peace and politics.
  • The Commonwealth: Mostly former parts of the British Empire, tied by history and language.
