Uganda

Eastern Africa, about 44,269,594 people

Who's In Charge

  • President: Yoweri Museveni, National Resistance Movement, since 29 January 1986 (Yoweri Museveni's record on Wikidata)

How It's Run

Yoweri Museveni has led Uganda since 1986. Limits on a president's terms and age were removed so he could go on, and the army stays deeply involved.

How Voting Works

Many parties compete, but human rights groups say opposition leaders have faced arrest and violence, and observers have doubted results. Museveni was declared the winner in January 2026 with about 72 percent, which critics dispute. Most seats in parliament go by first-past-the-post, and some are set aside for women, the army, young people and others.

  • Voting age: 18.
  • People vote about every 5 years.
  • Nobody has to vote.

Who Takes Part

  • About 55% of registered voters turned out in 2021, as officially reported.
  • Women hold about 35% of seats in parliament.

What Researchers Say

Different experts judge countries differently, so here are three.

  • V-Dem: Electoral Autocracy (2025)
  • Freedom House: 33 Out Of 100 (2025)
  • Bertelsmann: Moderate Autocracy (2025)

What Surveys Say

  • People here rate their lives 4.5 out of 10 (2025, World Happiness Report).
  • About 47% said they trust their national government (2020, Wellcome Global Monitor).

How It Got Here

Uganda won independence from Britain in 1962. Milton Obote ruled, then Idi Amin after a 1971 coup, then Obote again, and a civil war followed. Museveni's rebels took power in 1986.

Who It Stands With

  • African Union: Nearly every country in Africa, working together on peace and politics.
  • The Commonwealth: Mostly former parts of the British Empire, tied by history and language.
