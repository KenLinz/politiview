Uruguay

South America, about 3,461,734 people

Who's In Charge

  • President: Yamandú Orsi, Broad Front, since 1 March 2025 (Yamandú Orsi's record on Wikidata)

How It's Run

Voters elect a president and a General Assembly with two chambers. Uruguay is often ranked among the strongest democracies in Latin America.

How Voting Works

A president needs a majority, or there's a runoff. Seats are shared by party vote share, and voting is compulsory. Citizens can also force national referendums.

What Surveys Say

  • People here rate their lives 6.6 out of 10 (2025, World Happiness Report).
  • About 70% said they trust their national government (2020, Wellcome Global Monitor).

How It Got Here

Uruguay became independent in 1828, with Brazil and Argentina on either side. It built a strong welfare system early on, but an army dictatorship ruled from 1973 to 1985. Democracy came back and has stayed strong.
