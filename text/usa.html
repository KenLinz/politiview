United States

Northern America, about 328,239,523 people

Who's In Charge

  • President: Donald Trump, Republican Party, since 20 January 2025 (Donald Trump's record on Wikidata)

How It's Run

A president chosen through the electoral college runs the government, and a separately elected Congress writes the laws. Congress has two parts: the House, where bigger states get more seats, and the Senate, where every state gets two. Courts can strike down laws that break the constitution. It's a federal system, so all 50 states also have their own governors and laws.

How Voting Works

House members serve two years, senators six, and presidents four. Most seats go to whoever gets the most votes, which is called first-past-the-post. The president isn't chosen by the nationwide vote total but by the electoral college: each state gets a number of electors, and nearly all states give every elector to whoever wins that state. Each state runs its own elections, so the rules vary.

  • Voting age: 18.
  • People vote about every 2 years.
  • Nobody has to vote.

How It Got Here

Thirteen British colonies declared independence in 1776. They argued over how much power a central government should have, and in 1787 wrote the constitution still used today. Small and large states compromised: seats in one chamber by population, and equal seats in the other. The right to vote started narrow and grew over time, reaching Black men on paper in 1870, women in 1920, and enforced Black voting rights in 1965.
