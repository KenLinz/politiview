Venezuela

South America, about 28,515,829 people

Who's In Charge

  • Acting President: Delcy Rodriguez, United Socialist Party of Venezuela, since 3 January 2026 (Delcy Rodriguez's record on Wikidata)

How It's Run

On paper, Venezuela has an elected president and a National Assembly. In real life, its government has gathered up a lot of power. Many accuse it of controlling the courts, the army and election officials.

How Voting Works

Voters use machines, and the president wins with the most votes. In 2024, Nicolás Maduro was declared the winner without the detailed results being published, and the opposition published tallies showing he lost. Many countries rejected the official result.

What Surveys Say

  • People here rate their lives 5.8 out of 10 (2025, World Happiness Report).
  • About 31% said they trust their national government (2020, Wellcome Global Monitor).

How It Got Here

Simón Bolívar led the fight to break from Spain in the early 1800s. Venezuela grew rich from oil and was a stable democracy from 1958. Hugo Chávez won in 1998 promising to help the poor, and began a socialist project. After he died in 2013, the economy collapsed and millions of people left. In January 2026, a US military operation seized Maduro, and his vice president, Delcy Rodríguez, took over.
