Zambia

Eastern Africa, about 17,861,030 people

Who's In Charge

  • President: Hakainde Hichilema, United Party for National Development, since 24 August 2021 (Hakainde Hichilema's record on Wikidata)

How It's Run

Voters elect a president, who runs the government, along with a parliament. Zambia has seen several peaceful changes of power since 1991.

How Voting Works

A president needs over 50%, or there's a runoff. Opposition leader Hakainde Hichilema won in 2021 on his sixth try, and was re-elected in August 2026 with about 60 percent. Observers said the 2026 vote was free and open but not fair, citing use of state money and limits on opposition campaigns. The main challenger disputed the result.

  • Voting age: 18.
  • People vote about every 5 years.
  • Nobody has to vote.

Different experts judge countries differently, so here are three.

What Surveys Say

  • People here rate their lives 4.1 out of 10 (2025, World Happiness Report).
  • About 47% said they trust their national government (2020, Wellcome Global Monitor).

How It Got Here

Zambia won independence from Britain in 1964. Kenneth Kaunda led it until elections with many parties began in 1991, when he lost and stepped down.

Who It Stands With
