Zimbabwe

Eastern Africa, about 14,645,468 people

Who's In Charge

  • President: Emmerson Mnangagwa, Zimbabwe African National Union – Patriotic Front, since 24 November 2017 (Emmerson Mnangagwa's record on Wikidata)

How It's Run

A strong president runs the government. The ZANU-PF party has held power since independence in 1980, and the army plays a big part. In 2026, changes to the constitution were reported to make terms seven years long, with parliament rather than voters choosing future presidents.

How Voting Works

Elections are held, but observers have reported threats and unfair conditions, which the government rejects. The House of Assembly mixes 210 local winners with 60 extra seats for women, filled by party shares.

  • Voting age: 18.
  • People vote about every 5 years.
  • Nobody has to vote.

What Researchers Say

Different experts judge countries differently, so here are three.

  • V-Dem: Electoral Autocracy (2025)
  • Freedom House: 25 Out Of 100 (2025)
  • Bertelsmann: Hard-Line Autocracy (2025)

What Surveys Say

  • People here rate their lives 3.4 out of 10 (2025, World Happiness Report).
  • About 69% said they trust their national government (2020, Wellcome Global Monitor).

How It Got Here

A white minority government declared independence as Rhodesia in 1965, but other countries didn't accept it. After a war, Robert Mugabe led Zimbabwe from 1980 until the army pushed him out in 2017. Some called that a coup, and the army did not.

Who It Stands With

  • African Union: Nearly every country in Africa, working together on peace and politics.
